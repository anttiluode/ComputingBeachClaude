Beaches as attention

Sixteen channels carry key swells in from the west. Sixteen more carry teacher swells up from the harbours in the south. Where two channels cross, a small basin of sand feels both swells at once, and the interference between them moves its sand. Store pairs with strong swells, then ping a key faintly and listen at the harbours. How the sand responds decides what kind of memory the beach is.

Blue basins are dug deeper, ochre ones silted up. A magenta ring means the basin hit bedrock or the surface and can't move further. Squares on the west shore are the key, the bars in the south are what each harbour hears (ticks show what it should hear; magenta bars are wrong).

Two beaches and a puddle

Beach A stores x → y. Beach B stores y → z. Ping x into A, and A's harbours flow into B's inlets through a puddle. Can B answer z, two hops away? The puddle is the only fast nonlinearity on this page, and there are two kinds.

no puddle (straight through) overtopping: only what clears the crest spills over shoal and breaker: the wave grows ×3 as the water shallows, then breaks at a fixed height

The same thing in machine-learning words

A crossbar beach is a fast-weight memory: a matrix written by the signals passing through it. Linear attention and DeltaNet are built from exactly this, so none of it is new. What the beach adds is a reason each rule exists, and a picture of it.

The wave-equation version of these rules was measured in this repository: plain scour is gradient descent on the swell's own compliance (G6), and the habit rule gives one-exposure gradient steps (G7). On real hardware the crossbar is not a metaphor. Photonic weight banks put a tuned microring at each crossing, its weight set by its detuning. Phase-change photonic memories are written by strong light pulses and read by weak ones, and volume holograms store thousands of pages in one crystal, addressed by angle or wavelength.

What this page is, and isn't

Computed exactly

  • The algebra of an idealised crossbar beach, in crossbar.js. Each basin's sloshing amplitude is the sum of what its two channels pour in. Its sand moves with the time-averaged sloshing energy, but only above a mobility (Shields) threshold. It passes the key wave into the harbour channel in proportion to its depth, and its depth is bounded.
  • node tests/check_crossbar.mjs checks the claims on this page: habit sand writes exactly k vᵀ, plain sand writes that plus a uniform dig three times larger, pings move exactly no sand, habit writes commute and listening writes don't, and the delta rule stores 16 orthogonal keys exactly.

Assumed or left out

  • No wave equation here. Basins pass waves in one pass with no echoes. In the real stadium, echoes scrambled the read badly (G1: the sand held order 98% of the time, but pings read it back about 75% of the time).
  • Teacher swells arrive with the right phase. In the lossy stadium they don't (G5, G8): this page is the lossless limit.
  • The habit is already formed (habituated on random traffic). How it forms, and how it goes stale as the sand moves, is G7's result, not modelled here.
  • Not new as machine learning. It's a way to see it.
